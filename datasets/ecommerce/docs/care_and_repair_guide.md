# Care and Repair Guide

Good gear lasts for years when it is cleaned and stored properly. This guide covers the three things we are asked about most: tents, Emberhollow cast iron and Fernglow lanterns. It also explains when to stop repairing and start a warranty claim.

## Cleaning and drying a tent

Set the tent up in a shaded spot and brush off loose dirt and leaves with a soft brush. Wash the fly and body with a sponge, cool water and a little mild, unscented soap. Never use a washing machine, hot water, detergent with additives, or a dryer. These strip the waterproof coating and can weaken the seams.

Rinse well so no soap remains, then leave the tent pitched until it is completely dry. Drying matters more than any other step. A tent packed damp will grow mildew within days, and mildew damages the coating permanently. Wipe the poles and zips, and brush a little dry grit out of the zip teeth before packing.

For storage, keep the tent loosely packed in a breathable bag, not tightly rolled in its stuff sack, in a cool and dry room. Never store it in a garage or loft where heat swings are large.

## Small tent repairs you can do yourself

Tiny holes and snags in the fly can be patched with a self-adhesive repair patch. Clean the area, let it dry, round the corners of the patch and press it on firmly from the inside and the outside. A sticking zip can often be freed by cleaning the teeth and rubbing a little zip lubricant along them. A bent pole section can sometimes be straightened gently, but a cracked or splintered pole should be replaced, not taped.

## Re-seasoning Emberhollow cast iron

Seasoning is the thin layer of baked oil that makes cast iron non-stick and protects it from rust. If your pan looks dull, grey or patchy, or food starts to stick, it is time to re-season.

1. Wash the pan with hot water and a stiff brush. A little soap is fine for this step. Remove any rust with fine steel wool until the metal is bare and even.
2. Dry it completely, then warm it on a low burner for a few minutes to drive off hidden moisture.
3. Rub a very thin coat of a neutral cooking oil over every surface, inside and outside, including the handle. Then wipe it all off as if you made a mistake. A thin layer is the secret, because thick oil turns sticky.
4. Bake the pan upside down at 230 degrees Celsius for one hour, with a sheet of foil on the lower rack to catch drips. Let it cool in the oven.
5. Repeat the oiling and baking two or three times for a deep finish.

Day to day, wipe the pan while it is still warm, rinse with hot water, dry it on the burner and rub in a few drops of oil. Never soak it and never put it in a dishwasher.

## Charging a Fernglow lantern safely

Charge the lantern indoors, on a dry hard surface, away from heat and direct sun. Use the cable that came with it. Do not charge it in a closed bag or under bedding. A full charge takes about four hours, and the indicator turns from red to green when finished. Unplug it once it is green rather than leaving it connected for days.

Do not use a lantern with a cracked lens or a swollen casing. Do not open the battery compartment. Keep it away from water while charging, and store it at about half charge if you will not use it for months. In very cold weather, let it warm to room temperature before charging.

## When to repair and when to claim

Normal wear, small snags and routine cleaning are yours to handle with the steps above. Start a warranty claim instead when the problem comes from the product itself, for example a pole that bends under normal use, a seam that splits, a zip that fails, a lantern that will not hold a charge, or a Emberhollow pan that cracks. Tents and packs carry a 2 year warranty and Emberhollow cast iron carries a lifetime warranty.

Please do not attempt structural repairs on a product you plan to claim on. Photograph the problem first, keep your order id handy, and contact support. If the order is still within 30 days of delivery, you can ask for a refund instead of a repair, and the return label fee is waived for defects.
