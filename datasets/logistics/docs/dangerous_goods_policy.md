# Dangerous Goods Acceptance Policy

## Purpose and scope

This policy explains which dangerous goods Tallowgate Freight will carry, which it refuses outright, and what a shipper must do before a hazardous consignment reaches any of our six hubs (KRN, MAV, HLM, TRB, WYN and ORS). It applies to every domestic and cross-border lane and to all three carrier contracts (CC-17, CC-22 and CC-31). The rules are generic and follow the usual hazard class system. If you are unsure whether a product counts as dangerous goods, ask the operations desk before you book. The desk runs 24/7.

## Goods we always refuse

Some classes are never accepted, whatever the packaging or paperwork:

- **Class 1, explosives.** This covers fireworks, blasting caps, flares, ammunition and anything else designed to detonate or burn violently.
- **Class 6.2, infectious substances.** This covers biological samples, cultures and clinical waste that may carry a pathogen.
- **Class 7, radioactive material.** This covers any consignment that gives off measurable radiation, including sealed sources and contaminated equipment.

A refused consignment is not loaded. If it is discovered after pickup, it is held at the nearest hub and the shipper is told straight away. The shipper pays for storage and return.

## Goods accepted only with prior booking

The following classes may travel, but only when the shipper has booked them as dangerous goods and supplied the paperwork described below:

- **Class 3, flammable liquids**, such as paints, solvents, adhesives and fuels in approved containers.
- **Class 8, corrosives**, such as acid cleaners, battery fluid and descaling products.
- **Class 9, miscellaneous**, in limited quantities only. This includes lithium cells packed to standard, dry ice and magnetised items.

Quantities per package and per consignment are capped. The operations desk will confirm the cap for the product when you give notice. A consignment that exceeds the cap must be split or refused.

## Notice and paperwork

Give at least 48 hours of notice before the planned pickup for any accepted dangerous goods class. Notice made later than that may be declined, because hub staff need time to plan segregated storage and to brief the linehaul driver.

For every dangerous goods consignment the shipper must provide:

- a dangerous goods declaration signed by the shipper, naming the substance, the class, the quantity and the packing group;
- a safety data sheet for the product;
- an emergency contact who can be reached while the shipment is in transit;
- the shipment id on the declaration, so the hub can match paperwork to cargo.

A consignment that arrives without a complete declaration is set to status `held` until the paperwork is corrected.

## Packing and marking

Dangerous goods must be packed in containers approved for the class. Liquids need leak-proof inner packaging and absorbent material. Each package must carry the hazard label for its class and the orientation arrows where the product is liquid. Labels go on two adjacent sides together with the shipment id. Do not mix incompatible classes in one pallet. Dangerous goods pallets must follow the same height and weight limits as standard pallets.

## Handling at hubs

Accepted dangerous goods are stored apart from general freight and apart from incompatible classes. Hubs may refuse a transfer if the receiving hub cannot store the class, which is one reason a reroute can be refused. Hub staff trained in dangerous goods handling sign the movement at every scan.

## Incidents

If a package leaks, is damaged or is found to be misdeclared, the hub isolates it and calls the operations desk. The shipper is told at once and is responsible for cleanup costs arising from a misdeclaration. Incidents are reviewed by the claims team.

## Questions

For any question about whether a product can be shipped, contact the operations desk. Account managers can help with recurring dangerous goods arrangements during office hours, Monday to Friday, 7:00 to 19:00.
