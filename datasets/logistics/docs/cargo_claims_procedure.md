# Cargo Claims Procedure

This procedure explains how shippers lodge a claim for cargo that arrives damaged, arrives short or does not arrive at all. It is used by the operations desk and by the claims team. It applies to every consignment moved by Tallowgate Freight, whichever carrier contract it travels under.

## Who can claim

The claim must come from the shipper named on the booking, or from the consignee with the shipper's written agreement. A claim on behalf of a third party, such as an insurer, needs written authority from the shipper. The desk should confirm who is asking before it logs anything.

## Time limits

A claim must be lodged within 21 days of delivery. For cargo that never arrives, the 21 days run from the expected delivery date shown on the last `eta` record. The limit is the same on contracts CC-17, CC-22 and CC-31. Claims lodged after 21 days are normally declined, so the desk should encourage shippers to report problems early. The operations desk can log a claim 24 hours a day.

## Evidence required

The claims team asks for the following, and a claim is not reviewed until the evidence is complete:

- Photographs of the damaged goods, the packaging and the pallet, taken before anything is moved or repacked.
- The signed delivery note with any damage written on it.
- The packing list or commercial invoice showing the value and weight of the goods.
- The shipment id and contract code.

Consignees should keep damaged goods and packaging until the claim is settled, because the carrier may ask to inspect them. On contract CC-22 the consignee must also report visible damage within 48 hours of delivery.

## Minimum claim value

The minimum claim value is 25. Claims below that amount are not processed, and the shipper should be told so at the start rather than after evidence has been gathered.

## How liability is applied

Compensation is limited by the liability per kilogram on the consignment's contract: 8.50 per kilogram on CC-17, 12.00 per kilogram on CC-22 and 6.00 per kilogram on CC-31, applied to the gross weight of the affected goods on the delivery note. The payout is the lower of the proven value of the goods and the weight-based limit. Consequential loss is not covered. Shippers who bought declared-value or insured cover are paid against the declared value instead, up to the cover amount.

## Review and decision

The claims team acknowledges a complete claim within two working days and gives a decision within 10 working days. If more information is needed, the clock pauses until it arrives. The decision is sent through the customer portal. A shipper who disagrees can ask their account manager to request a second review, which must be requested within 14 days of the decision.

## What the desk should and should not say

The desk can explain the window, the evidence and the contract limit. It should not promise a payout, a decision date earlier than 10 working days or a figure above the liability limit. If the shipper has not yet received the consignment, explain that the 21 days have not started.
