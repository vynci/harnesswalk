# Packaging and Pallet Standards

## Why standards matter

Most cargo damage at Tallowgate Freight comes from poor packing, not from handling. These standards describe how a consignment must be prepared so it can pass through linehaul, hub sorting and final delivery without harm. They apply to all shipments under contracts CC-17, CC-22 and CC-31. Cargo that does not meet them may be refused, repacked at the shipper's cost, or accepted with reduced liability, as set out in the carrier contract summaries and the cargo claims procedure.

## Pallet dimensions and weight

- **Footprint:** the standard pallet is 1.2 metres by 1.0 metres. Cargo must not overhang the pallet edges.
- **Height:** a pallet may not exceed 1.8 metres in height, measured from the floor to the top of the load, including the pallet itself. Taller pallets do not fit our trailers' double-stack racking and are declined at pickup.
- **Weight:** a pallet may not exceed 800 kilograms gross. Heavier loads must be split across pallets or booked as heavy freight through your account manager.
- **Condition:** pallets must be sound, with no broken boards or protruding nails. Damaged pallets are replaced at the shipper's cost.

## Securing the load

Every pallet must be stretch wrapped on all four sides and across the top, with at least three layers of film that overlap the pallet base. Strapping is required for loads above 400 kilograms. Cartons should be stacked in a column or interlocked pattern, with the heaviest items at the bottom. Nothing may be stacked on top of a pallet that is not marked as stackable.

## Fragile and special goods

- **Glass and tile:** use corner boards on all four vertical edges, a rigid top sheet and a separate "this way up" marking. Glass should be packed upright, never flat.
- **Temperature-sensitive loads:** declare the temperature range at booking. Insulated packaging must hold the range for at least 48 hours beyond the expected delivery.
- **Liquids:** use leak-proof containers with absorbent material and place them on the lowest layer.
- **Dangerous goods:** follow the dangerous goods acceptance policy as well.

## Labelling

Every pallet needs a label on two adjacent sides showing the shipment id (for example TG-604118), the origin and destination, the number of pieces and the contact name at the delivery address. Handling marks such as "fragile", "keep dry" and "this way up" are added where they apply. Old labels must be removed or covered before new ones are put on.

## Documents on the pallet

Place the delivery note in a sealed plastic sleeve under the stretch wrap on one outer side. Do not tape it inside the load. For cross-border lanes, the customs documents travel separately with the driver, as described in the customs paperwork checklist.

## Inspection at pickup

The driver checks height, weight, wrapping and labels at pickup. A pallet that fails a check is either corrected on the spot, if quick, or refused. The driver records the result on the pickup scan. If the shipper accepts a pallet marked "pickup condition noted", any later claim for damage that relates to the noted fault may be reduced or rejected.

## Returnable pallets

Pallets exchanged on a like-for-like basis are noted on the delivery note. Unreturned pallets are charged at the rate in the contract summary.
