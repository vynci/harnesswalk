# Brannock Highlands Guide

The Brannock Highlands are a range of hills reached by regional rail from Mirelake. They are quiet, walking-led and far from the ferry harbours, so most visitors stay for several nights and plan their days around the trails.

## Getting there

Trains run from Mirelake to the Brannock stop throughout the year. A typical journey from Mirelake takes under an hour. Hotels in the hills do not run shuttles, so travellers arriving by train should arrange a short taxi or walk from the stop before booking.

## Where to stay

Brannock Lodge (HT-21) is the only partner hotel in the hills. Check-in is from 16:00. Breakfast is not included, so guests should budget for a meal out. The lodge has free parking and no accessible rooms, so travellers who need step-free access should choose a town hotel instead. The lodge's cancellation terms are non-refundable, and the desk makes that clear before any booking is confirmed.

## Trails and the winter season

The trails close above the snow line from 1 December each year. Before then the lower paths are open, and the ridge walks are open until the first heavy snow. The desk checks the snow-line notice on the day of booking. In winter the lower valley paths remain the safest choice, and travellers should pack a warm layer, waterproof boots and a head torch for the short afternoons.

## Worked example: a three-night walking break

A couple arrives on a 09:05 rail connection and checks in after 16:00. They walk the valley loop on the first full day, the ridge path on the second if the snow line allows, and take the train back on the third morning. Because breakfast is not included, they book a packed lunch for the walk and eat breakfast at the stop café.

## Questions and answers

**Does the lodge have an accessible room?** No. Travellers who need one should stay in Mirelake or Port Ellery.

**Is breakfast included?** No, at Brannock Lodge it is an extra.

**Can I cancel a booking at the lodge?** The lodge's terms are non-refundable, so check the terms before confirming.

**When do the trails close?** Above the snow line from 1 December; check the notice before a winter walk.
