# Copperline Cup Rules

The Copperline Cup is the league's knockout competition, played alongside the autumn season. It is open to all eight clubs, from both the premier and championship divisions, and it is meant to be a fun, dramatic addition to the calendar. These rules explain how the draw, the ties, extra time, penalties and discipline work.

## Format and Entry

All eight league clubs enter the cup automatically. There are no byes, and there are no entry fees. The competition has three rounds: the quarter-finals, the semi-finals and the final. Every tie is a single match, so there are no second legs. The winner of a tie goes through to the next round and the loser is out of the cup, though the club's league fixtures are not affected.

Clubs from the two divisions can be drawn against each other from the very first round. The draw is made by the league office in front of at least two club captains, and the results are published on the fixtures page and sent to the captains.

## Home Advantage

For quarter-finals and semi-finals, the club drawn first plays at home. If both clubs share a venue, the league office chooses a neutral venue from the venue guide and tells both clubs at least seven days before the match. The final is always played at a single venue chosen by the league. In 2026 the final is on 2026-12-19 at Harbour Park.

## Match Length

Cup matches follow the league rulebook: two halves of 40 minutes with rolling substitutions, and a matchday squad of at most 16 players. Kick-off times are local. Squads must be submitted on the team sheet before kick-off, exactly as in a league match, and only players who are properly registered with the club may be named.

## Extra Time and Penalties

A cup tie cannot end in a draw. If the score is level after the 80 minutes of normal time, the match goes straight to extra time. Extra time is two halves of 10 minutes, with a short break of two minutes for water and tactics, and no break at all if the referee decides the match is running late. Substitutions continue to roll, and the squad limit does not change.

If the score is still level after extra time, the tie is decided by penalties. Each team takes five penalties in turn. If the score is still level after five each, the shoot-out continues one penalty each at a time until one team is ahead after both have taken the same number of kicks. Only players who finished the match on the pitch may take a penalty, and a goalkeeper may be changed only if the starting goalkeeper is injured.

## Cards and Discipline

Discipline in the cup follows the Discipline Code, and cards carry over between competitions. Yellow cards received in the cup add to a player's season total, and a player who reaches five yellow cards across the league and the cup serves a one-match ban. A red card carries a ban of at least two matches, and the ban is served in whichever competition the club plays next, cup or league.

A booking received in a penalty shoot-out counts as a normal yellow card. A player who is sent off during extra time cannot be replaced, and the club plays on with one fewer player, including during the shoot-out.

## Postponements

Weather decisions follow the league policy: the league calls off a match by 10:00 on match day if the pitch is unplayable. A postponed cup tie must be played within seven days. If it cannot be played in that time, the league office may order the tie to be moved to a neutral venue, and in rare cases may decide it by a coin toss held in front of both captains.

## The Final and the Trophy

The final is a showpiece, with a longer build-up and a full half-hour of warm-up on the pitch. Both clubs can bring up to 40 supporters into the main stand without a ticket, and the league sells the remaining places. The winning captain receives the Copperline Cup, which stays with the club until the following season's quarter-finals. The runner-up receives a plaque, and a medal goes to every member of the match squad on both sides.

## Questions and Disputes

Captains can raise a question about the draw, a result or a ruling with the league office, Monday to Thursday, 18:00 to 21:00. The help desk can explain these rules and read out the cup dates, but it cannot change a ruling or settle a dispute. The league office has the final say on all cup matters.
