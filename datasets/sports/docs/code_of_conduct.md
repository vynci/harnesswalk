# Code of Conduct

The Copperline Community League is run by volunteers for players, families and neighbours. Everyone who takes part, on the pitch or beside it, agrees to this code. It applies to players, coaches, captains, club officials, match officials, volunteers and spectators at every league and cup match, at training sessions held at league venues, and in any league message group or online space.

## Our Principles

Football is better when people enjoy it. We expect every person connected with a match to follow four principles.

1. Respect the match officials. Referees and assessors are volunteers who give up their weekends. Their decisions are final on the day.
2. Respect opponents and teammates. We win and lose as sportspeople and shake hands afterwards.
3. Respect the venue. Leave changing rooms, touchlines and car parks tidier than you found them.
4. Respect the game. No cheating, no time wasting, no intentional injury.

## Players

Players must accept the referee's decisions without arguing, dissent or gestures. Only the captain may speak to the referee about a decision, and only calmly and at a natural break in play.

The following are never acceptable and are treated as misconduct: abusive, discriminatory or threatening language, spitting, violent conduct, deliberately injuring an opponent, and attempting to influence a decision by pretending to be fouled. A player who is sent off must leave the technical area and the pitch surround immediately and may not take part in the rest of the match in any role.

A booking for dissent or persistent foul play counts as a yellow card and carries over toward the league's card accumulation rules. The details of bans are set out in the Discipline Code.

## Coaches, Captains and Club Officials

Coaches and officials are responsible for the behaviour of their whole club on matchday, including supporters who travel with them. Each club names one person as its matchday conduct lead, and the referee will speak to that person first if there is a problem on the touchline.

Coaches must stay inside the technical area, which is marked by the home club before kick-off. They may not enter the pitch without the referee's permission, except to attend to an injured player. Captains are asked to remind their squad of this code before the first match of the season and again after any incident.

## Spectators and Families

Spectators are guests of the home club. Encouragement is welcome; criticism of officials, opponents or young players is not. Spectators must stay behind the barrier or marked line, keep dogs on leads, and follow any instruction from a venue steward or the referee.

Children are present at many matches. Language and behaviour should always be suitable for them. Anyone who is abusive from the sideline may be asked to leave the venue, and the referee may pause or abandon a match until they have gone.

## Reporting a Concern

Anyone can report a conduct concern to the league office through the support form on the league's help page, or captains can raise it during office hours, Monday to Thursday, 18:00 to 21:00. A report should say what happened, which match it was, roughly when, and who was involved. Reports are handled by a small conduct panel of three volunteers who were not involved in the match.

The help desk can explain how to report but cannot decide a conduct case, hand out a sanction or comment on an open report. Match officials also submit a short written report after any sending off or abandoned match, usually within two days.

## Sanctions

The conduct panel decides sanctions in proportion to what happened. The range runs from a written warning and a talk with the club's conduct lead, through match bans for individuals and a ban on a spectator attending matches, up to a points deduction for a club whose supporters or officials repeatedly break the code. A serious or repeated breach can lead to a club being removed from a competition.

Clubs have the right to appeal a sanction to the league office. An appeal must be made in writing within five days of the decision and must say which part of the finding the club disagrees with. While an appeal is being considered, a match ban for a sending off still applies.

## Looking After Each Other

If a player, official or spectator is injured or taken ill, play stops and the safety of the person comes first. Every venue has a first aid kit, and clubs are asked to have at least one trained first aider at each home match. Safeguarding concerns about a child or vulnerable adult must be passed to the league office straight away and are not to be investigated by the club on its own.

Following this code is how a small league stays friendly. Thank you for doing your part.
