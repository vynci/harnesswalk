# Referee and Assessor Guide

This guide explains how match officials are appointed and what they must do on a Copperline Community League matchday. It is written for referees and assessors, and captains may read it to understand what to expect from the officials.

## Appointments

The league office appoints one referee to every match. Appointments are published with the fixture list, and a referee who cannot attend must tell the office as early as possible so that a replacement can be found. Referees may not officiate a match involving a club they have played for in the current season.

Each matchday also has one assessor, who watches a selection of the day's matches and gives feedback to referees. The assessor does not take part in decisions during a match and cannot overturn a referee's call. Assessors rotate between venues across the season so that every referee is observed at least twice.

## Match length and conduct of play

Matches are played as two halves of 40 minutes with rolling substitutions. The referee keeps time and adds stoppage time at the end of each half for injuries, delays and substitutions that take too long. Referees check the team sheets 60 minutes before kick-off and confirm that every named player is registered with the club and that no squad exceeds 16 players.

## Recording cards

Every caution and every sending-off is recorded on the match report. A yellow card is a caution. Five yellow cards in a season earn a one-match ban. A red card, whether straight or for a second caution, carries a ban of at least two matches. The referee writes the player's id, the minute and a short reason for each card. Referees do not announce ban lengths on the day, because the league office confirms them after reviewing the report.

## Match reports

The match report must be returned to the league office within 24 hours of the final whistle. It must contain the final score, the scorers by player id, all cards, any injury that stopped play, and any incident involving spectators or officials. Late reports delay the updated league table, so referees who expect to be late should tell the office in advance.

## The 10:00 weather inspection

On a day when the weather may put a pitch at risk, the referee and a club representative inspect the pitch. The league makes its postponement call by 10:00 on match day. The referee reports the pitch condition to the office, which then tells both captains and the venue. If the call is to play, the referee has the final say on the pitch during the match and may abandon a game if conditions become unsafe. An abandoned match is rearranged by the office.

## Assessor duties

The assessor files a short written note for each referee observed, covering control of the match, handling of cards, communication with captains and positioning. Notes are shared with the referee within a week. Assessors also check that match reports arrive on time and that the venue meets the minimum standard set in the Venue Guide.

## Questions

Referees and assessors contact the league office Monday to Thursday, 18:00 to 21:00. The volunteer help desk can answer rules questions from the league documents, but it does not decide disciplinary cases or change an appointment.
