# Passwords and Account Lockouts

Strong passwords and careful handling of them protect your work, your colleagues and the studio's shared files. This page sets out how passwords work at Pebblebrook Studio and what to do when you are locked out.

## Resetting a password

Passwords are reset on the self-service account portal. A reset takes about 5 minutes. Go to the portal, choose the option to reset your password, and follow the steps it shows. You do not need to contact the IT desk for a normal reset.

If the portal does not work, open a ticket through the support form and describe what happened, including the time and the message you saw. The desk answers new tickets within 4 working hours.

## What makes a good password

A password must be at least 14 characters long. Longer is better. A short phrase of random words is easier to remember than a complicated string of symbols, and it is harder for attackers to guess. Do not reuse a password you have used on any other site or service, and do not write your password on paper near your desk.

## How often to change it

You must change your password every 12 months. The portal reminds you a few weeks before the change is due. You may change it sooner at any time, for example if you think someone else has seen it.

## Account lockouts

After several failed sign-in attempts, your account is locked for security. A locked account unlocks by itself after 15 minutes. You do not need to call anyone or raise a ticket. Wait the 15 minutes, then try again. If you have forgotten your password, the portal can reset it at any point, even while the account is locked.

Repeated lockouts can signal a problem, such as an old password saved on a phone or an app still trying to sign in. If you keep getting locked out, check your phone and any other devices for saved passwords and remove the old ones before you try again.

## Never share your password

Nobody at the IT desk will ever ask for your password. The desk never asks for a password by chat, email or phone, and it never asks you to read one out or type one into a message. If anyone asks you for a password, claiming to be from IT, a supplier or a colleague, do not give it to them. Report the request through the support form so the desk can check it.

Do not share your password with anyone at the studio either. Each person has their own account, and the logs depend on that.

## If you think your password has leaked

If you think a password has been seen by someone else, reset it straight away on the account portal. Then open a ticket through the support form so the desk can check whether anything else needs attention. Do not wait until the next change date.
