# How distributions are split

When a Hollowmere fund sells an asset or receives income and decides to pay it out, the amount is shared out in a set order. This order is called the distribution waterfall. This guide explains the steps, how to read the split shown in a distribution notice, and what the split means for an investor's remaining cost in the fund. It is general information about how the funds work and is not tax advice.

## Step one: return of capital

The first part of every distribution goes back to investors as return of capital, until investors have received back all the capital they have paid in through capital calls. Return of capital is not a profit. It simply gives investors back money they put in. In the distribution notice it is shown as a percent of the total distribution.

## Step two: the preferred return

Once capital has been returned, the next amount goes to investors as a preferred return, up to the fund's hurdle rate. The hurdle is an annual percentage on paid-in capital. It is 6 percent for HCP-02, 7 percent for HCP-03, and 8 percent for HCP-01 and HCP-04. The manager earns no carry until investors have received this return.

## Step three: catch-up and carry

After the preferred return, the manager receives a catch-up, which brings its share to the agreed carry percentage of total profits. Carry is 20 percent for HCP-01 and HCP-04, 15 percent for HCP-02, and 12.5 percent for HCP-03. After the catch-up, further profits are shared according to the carry percentage.

## How a notice shows the split

A distribution notice states the amount per unit, the record date, the payment date, and the split between return of capital and gains. The two shares add up to 100 percent. The notice shows the split for that distribution only. A fund that is still returning capital will show a high return of capital share. A fund in its later years, where capital has largely been returned, shows a higher share of gains.

## Example: HCP-02

The HCP-02 distribution notice shows a split of 70 percent return of capital and 30 percent gains. On a distribution of 4.00 EUR per unit, 2.80 EUR per unit is return of capital and 1.20 EUR per unit is gains. The investor's remaining paid-in capital, called the cost basis in the fund, is reduced by the 2.80 EUR per unit that was returned.

## What return of capital means for remaining cost

Because return of capital lowers an investor's remaining cost basis in the fund, it also lowers the amount of paid-in capital that still has to come back before the fund moves on to the preferred return and carry. The fund's reports show each investor's paid-in capital, returned capital and remaining cost on the quarterly statement.

## Questions the desk cannot answer

How a distribution or its parts are taxed depends on the investor's own situation and country. The assistant and the investor relations desk cannot give tax, investment or legal advice. Investors should take the notice and their statement to their own tax adviser. The desk can confirm the figures in a notice, Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00.
