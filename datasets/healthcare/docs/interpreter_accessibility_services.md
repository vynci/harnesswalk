# Interpreter and Accessibility Services

Brindlemoor Family Clinic wants every patient to be able to understand their care and get through the door comfortably. This page describes the interpreter, communication and accessibility services available at the north, riverside and eastgate sites, and how to ask for them.

## Free interpreter services

Spoken-language interpreters are free for patients. They can join an appointment in person, by phone or by video, depending on the language and the site. To arrange one, tell the clinic when you book through the patient portal or the front desk, and name the language you need.

Please ask at least 3 business days before your appointment so the clinic can schedule an interpreter. If you cannot give that much notice, still ask: the clinic will try to use a phone interpreter service, which is often available at short notice. You may bring a family member or friend for support, but the clinic will not ask a child to interpret, and clinical staff may still book a professional interpreter to make sure information is accurate.

## Sign language interpreting

Sign language interpreters are available on request. Because they are in high demand, the clinic asks for at least 5 business days notice. Tell us the sign language you use and the date of your appointment, and we will confirm the booking through the patient portal or by phone. If your appointment is moved or cancelled, we will tell the interpreter at the same time, so please let us know as early as possible.

## Printed and written materials

Many of the clinic's forms and guides are available in alternative formats:

- Large print for patients with low vision.
- Easy-read versions of the registration guide, the patient FAQ and the Patient Rights and Responsibilities page, written in short sentences with pictures.
- Plain-language summaries of preparation notes, such as fasting instructions before a blood test.

Ask for these at the front desk or through the patient portal. Most are ready within 3 business days. The patient portal also supports larger text and a high-contrast display.

## Quiet rooms and sensory support

Some patients find waiting rooms loud or bright. Each site can offer a quiet room or a shorter wait where possible. For counselling and other longer visits, you can ask for a quiet room in advance. For example, a patient booked for a counselling intake can request one when they complete the short intake form in the patient portal. Staff will also explain what will happen during a visit before it starts, if that helps.

## Physical access

All three sites have step-free entrances and accessible toilets. The north site has a free car park behind the building with 40 spaces, including accessible bays. The riverside site offers two hours of free street parking on Willowbank Row. The eastgate site has a paid garage next door at 3 dollars an hour, with a lift to street level. If you use a wheelchair, mobility aid or assistance animal, tell the front desk when you book, so staff can plan the room and the route. Service animals are welcome at every site.

## Support for patients with cognitive or communication needs

Patients can ask for longer appointment times, written summaries after a visit and a support person to stay with them throughout. If you find phone calls difficult, you can ask for contact through the patient portal only, and the clinic will note this on your file.

## How to request an accommodation

1. Open the patient portal and choose the accommodation request option when booking, or ask the front desk.
2. Say what you need: language, sign language, format, quiet room, extra time or physical access.
3. The clinic confirms the arrangement by portal message, usually within 2 business days.

Requests are kept on your file so you do not have to repeat them at every visit. If something was not in place on the day, tell the front desk or send feedback in the patient portal, and the site manager will follow up within 10 business days.

## What this page does not cover

This page describes access services only. It does not give medical advice. For urgent symptoms, contact emergency services immediately. For clinical questions, speak with your own clinician or use the nurse advice line through the patient portal.
