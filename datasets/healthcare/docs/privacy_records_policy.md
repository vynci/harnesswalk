# Privacy and Records Request Policy

Brindlemoor Family Clinic keeps patient information private and shares it only with the patient or with someone the patient has authorised in writing. This policy explains who can ask for records, how to ask, how long it takes and what the clinic will never do. It applies to all three sites: north, riverside and eastgate.

## What counts as a record

A record is anything the clinic holds about your care. That includes visit notes, referral letters, vaccination history, lab results, imaging reports, prescription history and billing statements. Appointment details are part of your record too, which is why front-desk staff and the chat assistant cannot discuss another person's appointments.

## Who may request records

The patient may request their own records at any time. A person acting for the patient may request them only with the patient's written consent, or with a legal authority such as a guardianship or power of attorney document. For children under 16, a parent or legal guardian may request records, and the clinician may limit what is released if it protects the child.

Friends, neighbours, employers and other relatives cannot request someone else's records, even in an emergency. In a real emergency, call emergency services and they will contact the clinic through the proper channels.

## How to make a request

Records requests are made in one of two ways:

1. Through the patient portal, using the "Request my records" form.
2. At the front desk of any site, during opening hours.

Both routes need an identity check. Online, the portal login serves as the first check. At the front desk, staff ask for photo identification and compare it with the details on file. The request form asks which records you want and the date range, and whether you want a printed copy, a portal download or a copy sent to another clinician.

## How long it takes

The clinic answers every records request within 10 business days. Simple requests, such as a vaccination history, are often ready sooner. If a request is large or needs a clinician to review it first, the clinic tells you within the 10 business days and gives a new date.

Lab results are a separate, faster route. They appear in the patient portal 3 to 5 business days after the test. You do not need a records request to see them.

## What the clinic never does

The clinic never shares records by chat, by phone message or by email. This includes the chat assistant on the patient portal: it can explain the process and point you to the form, but it cannot read out, summarise or send any part of a record. Staff will not confirm over the phone whether someone is a patient of the clinic.

The chat assistant also does not interpret lab results or visit notes. Questions about what a result means go to your own clinician, in an appointment booked through the patient portal.

## Personal details in chat

If you type a health number, an email address or a phone number into the chat, the clinic's systems replace it with a placeholder before the assistant sees it. The assistant does not store those details and will not repeat a health number it did not receive from you in that message. Changes to your contact details are made in the patient portal or at the front desk, not in chat.

## Corrections and complaints

If you believe something in your record is wrong, ask for a correction through the patient portal or the front desk. A clinician reviews the request and either amends the record or adds your statement beside it. If you are unhappy with how your information was handled, ask the front desk for the privacy contact form. The clinic acknowledges complaints within 3 business days.
