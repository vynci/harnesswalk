# Patient Rights and Responsibilities

Brindlemoor Family Clinic wants every visit to be safe, respectful and clear. This page explains what patients can expect from the clinic and what the clinic asks in return. It applies at all three sites: north, riverside and eastgate. The same standards apply whether you book through the patient portal, at the front desk or on the phone.

## Your right to privacy

Your health information belongs to you. Clinic staff only look at your file when they need it to care for you or to run the clinic. We do not share your records with family members, employers, friends or neighbours unless you give written consent or the law requires it.

The chat assistant follows the same rule. It masks personal details such as health numbers, emails and phone numbers before they are processed, and it cannot show you anyone else's appointments or records. Records requests are handled through the patient portal or the front desk, with an identity check, and are answered within 10 business days.

## Your right to respectful care

You can expect to be greeted politely, addressed by the name you prefer and listened to without judgement. Staff will explain what is happening in plain language and will answer questions as best they can. If you feel you were not treated respectfully, you can tell the front desk or send feedback through the patient portal. Feedback is reviewed by the site manager, and a reply is sent within 10 business days.

You may bring a support person to any appointment. If a clinician needs to speak with you alone, they will explain why.

## Your right to an interpreter

If English is not your first language, or if you are deaf or hard of hearing, you have the right to a free interpreter. Ask for one when you book through the patient portal or the front desk, ideally at least 3 business days before the visit so the clinic can arrange it. Family members may stay with you, but the clinic will not ask a child to interpret. More detail is in the Interpreter and Accessibility Services page.

## Your right to a second opinion

You may ask for a second opinion about a diagnosis or a treatment plan at any time. Your clinician will explain your options, help with a referral where one is needed and share the information the other clinician requires, with your consent. Asking for a second opinion will not affect how you are treated at Brindlemoor.

## Your right to understand costs

Fees are published in the Billing FAQ. An uninsured standard visit costs 85 dollars and an extended visit costs 130 dollars. Balances over 200 dollars can be spread over up to 6 monthly instalments. You can ask the front desk for a fee estimate before a visit, and you can ask for a copy of your statement at any time.

## Your responsibilities

To keep the clinic running well for everyone, we ask patients to:

- Keep contact details up to date. Update your phone number, email and address in the patient portal or at the front desk, so reminders and results reach the right person.
- Arrive 10 minutes before your appointment so check-in does not shorten your visit. Late arrivals may be asked to rebook if the clinician cannot safely run over.
- Give 24 hours notice of cancellations or changes. Cancelling or moving an appointment with less than 24 hours notice may carry a 25 dollar late cancellation fee. After three missed appointments in 12 months, bookings for the next 3 months go through the front desk.
- Share accurate information about your health, medications and allergies, and tell the clinician if anything changes.
- Treat staff and other patients with respect. Abuse, threats or discrimination are not tolerated and may lead to a patient being asked to leave.

## How to raise a concern

Start with the front desk at your site, who can often fix things on the spot. If you are not satisfied, send a message through the patient portal and ask for the site manager. Your concern will be acknowledged within 2 business days and answered within 10 business days. Raising a concern never affects your care.

## Where this page does not help

This page explains general rights and responsibilities. It does not give medical advice. For urgent symptoms, contact emergency services immediately. For clinical questions, speak with your own clinician or use the nurse advice line through the patient portal.
